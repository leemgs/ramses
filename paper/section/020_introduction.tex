\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{M}{ODEL} serving becomes a memory-systems problem when parameters and key--value state exceed accelerator memory. Requests then couple GPU video RAM (VRAM), host dynamic RAM (DRAM), NVMe bandwidth, queueing, and synchronization. A policy that helps one configuration can hurt another because the active bottleneck changes with working-set demand, reuse, concurrency, and transfer direction.

This issue matters for industrial analytics such as inspection, predictive maintenance, and digital-twin support, where sustained availability and energy budgets are important. Our evidence, however, is a laboratory single-node evaluation driven partly by a parameterized synthetic arrival trace; it is not a factory field trial or a hard-real-time control-loop evaluation. We consequently report deadline misses only as Quality of Service (QoS), make no 2~ms or Time-Sensitive Networking compliance claim, and do not map request misses to IEC~61508 Safety Integrity Levels.

Prior systems optimize paging, GPU--CPU offload, or request scheduling. RAMSES instead coordinates the VRAM--DRAM--NVMe path within one node while observing occupancy separately from actual bidirectional traffic. The design uses a lightweight state machine to choose admission, prefetch, and eviction actions rather than treating an analytical boundary as a guarantee.

The central research question is not whether another offload implementation can
reduce memory use, but \emph{when coordination across all three tiers changes
end-to-end behavior and when physical limits make that coordination
ineffective}. We answer it through a model--mechanism--evidence chain: the model
identifies an operating regime, the controller changes a named set of actions,
and the evaluation tests both the benefit and its predicted boundary.

The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{A falsifiable operating model.} The model separates resident demand
from actual bidirectional traffic and accounts for overlap, queueing, reuse, and
synchronization. Two dimensionless pressures predict a coordination-sensitive
region and capacity- and bandwidth-limited regions where RAMSES should provide
little or negative benefit.
\item \emph{A regime-to-action controller.} RAMSES maps those observable
pressures to admission, prefetch, and eviction actions using a specified
200~ms sampling interval, 5\% hysteresis, and three-sample switching rule. This
mapping, rather than its component mechanisms in isolation, is tested by the
policy-off ablation.
\item \emph{A deployable single-node realization.} GPU preallocation, NUMA-local
host allocation, and asynchronous block-aligned transfer are combined behind
an \texttt{LD\_PRELOAD} layer for unmodified PyTorch applications. We do not
claim a hardened GPUDirect Storage or multi-node data path.
\item \emph{Boundary-seeking empirical evidence.} The evaluation crosses models
and pressure regimes, includes a controller-disabled counterfactual and
parameter sweeps, and reports absolute tails, uncertainty, failures, and GPU
energy. Reported maxima are configuration-specific effects, not guarantees.
\end{itemize}

The scope is deliberately narrow: RAMSES supports best-effort single-node inference. Multi-node fabrics, plant integration, certified energy measurement, real-time guarantees, and functional-safety qualification remain future work.
